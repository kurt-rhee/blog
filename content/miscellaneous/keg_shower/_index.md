# Keg Shower



## Intro

I'm anti-plastic.  Don't get me wrong, in many cases it is a miracle material which can't easily be replaced with an alternative, but I think that it is mostly an over-used material because companies optimize for "cheap".  I generally prefer durable products, since they create less waste and I get to reduce my mental load over time since I don't have to think about replacing them ever, even if they come at a higher intial cost.  Often this means I buy products that reduce the usage of plastic components.

I also prefer products that don't cause pacific garbage patch levels of environmental catastrophe, that don't support oil companies, and that aren't endocrine disruptors.  

Anyways, I have been trying to reduce the amount of plastic that touches the water I drink, cook with, or spray on myself. 

## Problem Statement
I wanted a pressurizable portable shower for surfing and camping that I could use without running water through a plastic solar shower bag, plastic pump sprayer, or a long plastic hose sitting in the sun. 

The yakima road shower is a close fit, but uses a plastic hose and is very expensive. 

## The Idea

A keg is a non-plastic pressurizable container.

## The Solution

The build uses a 2.5 gallon Torpedo all-stainless ball lock keg with the stock stainless dip tubes installed.

The liquid post feeds the shower hose. The gas post gets air from a bicycle pump through a ball-lock quick disconnect and a tank filler valve. The bike pump adds a little pressure to the headspace in the keg, and that pressure pushes water up the liquid dip tube and out through the nozzle.  Its not a totally plastic free setup (o-rings, etc.) but its close enough.

In other words:

* Water goes in the keg.
* Air pressure goes in the gas side.
* Water comes out the liquid side.

That is the whole system.

## Parts

The core of the build is a [2.5 gallon Torpedo ball lock keg](https://morebeer.com/products/torpedo-keg-og-classic-ball-lock-double-rubber-corny-25-gal). 

From MoreBeer:

* 1 Torpedo 2.5 gallon ball lock keg.
* 1 Torpedo barbed ball-lock quick disconnect kit with beverage-out and gas-in disconnects.
* 4 to 6 small stainless hose clamps.

From elsewhere:

* 10 feet of 1/4 inch ID beverage / gas tubing.
* 1 Dixon `TFV2HB` tank filler valve with a 1/4 inch hose barb.
* 1 bicycle pump with a pressure gauge.
* 1 Gilmour Adjustable Twist Brass Heavy-Duty Hose Nozzle.
* 1 Midland `30469` brass adapter, 1/4 inch hose barb x 3/4 inch male garden hose thread.

The exact fittings matter more than they usually do in a project like this. The Torpedo barbed ball-lock quick disconnects use 1/4 inch hose barbs, so I standardized the whole build around 1/4 inch ID tubing. That avoids a pile of extra reducers and adapters.

A barbed ball-lock quick disconnect is just a fitting that snaps onto a keg post and has a ridged hose barb on the other side. Push the tubing over the barb, clamp it, and move on with your life.

## Assembly

The liquid side is the shower side.

I clamped the outlet hose onto the beverage-out ball-lock quick disconnect, then clamped the other end of that hose onto the 1/4 inch hose-barb side of the Midland brass garden-hose adapter. The Gilmour nozzle threads onto the 3/4 inch male garden-hose-thread side of that adapter.

The garden hose thread connection still needs a normal rubber hose washer. Without the washer, it will leak, because garden hose thread is not a tapered pipe thread.

The gas side is the pump side.

I left the stock gas post installed on the keg. Then I attached the gas-in ball-lock quick disconnect to a short length of tubing and clamped the Dixon tank filler valve onto the other end. The bicycle pump connects directly to the valve stem on that filler valve.

I only snap the gas quick disconnect onto the keg when I want to add pressure. The rest of the time, the keg is just a little stainless tank with water in it.

## Use

To use it, fill the keg with water and leave some headspace for air. Close the lid and make sure it is fully seated. Snap the gas quick disconnect and tank-filler-valve hose onto the gas post, connect the bike pump, and pump in a small amount of air pressure.

Then open the nozzle.

When the flow drops, add a little more air with the bike pump or a hand-held air compressor. When you are done, disconnect the gas quick disconnect, pull the pressure relief valve, empty the keg, and let it dry.


## Things I Like About It

The best part of this build is that it uses the stock corny keg internals. There is no silicone dip tube, no drilled lid, and no permanent modification to the keg. If I want to switch the keg back to standard ball-lock use later, I can.

I also like that the parts are ordinary. Ball-lock kegs are common. Quick disconnects are common. Hose clamps are common. Bike pumps are common. If a fitting wears out, it can be replaced without redesigning the whole thing.

The brass nozzle is also nicer than I expected for this use. A lot of camp shower heads are designed to be lightweight, which often means they feel like they are one year of sunshine away from becoming trash. The Gilmour nozzle doesnt't have a lot of features, but it is simple, metal and durable which is one of my stated values.

## Pressure

This is still a pressurized vessel, so it deserves some respect.

Use a bike pump with a pressure gauge, add pressure gradually, and use the pressure relief valve when you are done.

## Conclusion

It works and my wife now has hot showers to look forward to right after surfing which makes her more likely to come with me to go surfing on cold mornings which is a big win.
